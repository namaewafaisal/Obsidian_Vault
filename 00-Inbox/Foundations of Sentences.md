# Subject Verb Object (SVO)

## Core Idea

English is strongly based on word order.

Main structure:

```text
Subject + Verb + Object
````

Example:

```text
I eat apples.
```

| Part   | Function |
| ------ | -------- |
| I      | Subject  |
| eat    | Verb     |
| apples | Object   |

English meaning changes heavily based on position.

```text
Dog bites man.
Man bites dog.
```

Same words.
Different meaning because of order.

---

# Subject

## Definition

The subject is:

* who performs the action
* who experiences the state
* what the sentence is about

---

# Types of Subjects

## 1. Noun Subject

```text
Faizal studies.
The cat slept.
```

---

## 2. Pronoun Subject

```text
He runs.
They laughed.
```

---

## 3. Noun Phrase Subject

A group of words acting as one noun.

```text
The tall boy won.
My younger sister sings.
```

Entire phrase is subject.

---

## 4. Gerund Subject

Verb acting as noun.

```text
Swimming is fun.
Reading improves knowledge.
```

---

## 5. Infinitive Subject

```text
To travel is expensive.
To learn English takes time.
```

Less common in casual speech.

---

## 6. Clause Subject

Entire clause becomes subject.

```text
What he said is true.
Why she left remains unknown.
```

---

# Subject Rules

## Rule 1 — English Usually Requires Explicit Subject

Wrong:

```text
Is raining.
Very beautiful.
```

Correct:

```text
It is raining.
It is very beautiful.
```

English normally demands:

* subject
* verb

---

## Rule 2 — Imperatives Omit Subject

Commands remove visible subject.

```text
Sit down.
Open the door.
Listen carefully.
```

Hidden subject:

```text
you
```

---

## Rule 3 — Subject Can Be Dummy/Fake

English sometimes inserts grammatical subjects.

### Dummy "it"

```text
It is raining.
It is hot.
It seems impossible.
```

"It" may not refer to real object.

---

### Existential "there"

```text
There is a problem.
There are many students.
```

Real subject comes later.

---

## Rule 4 — Subject Controls Verb Agreement

```text
He runs.
They run.
```

Verb changes depending on subject.

This becomes important later in Subject-Verb Agreement.

---

# Verb

## Definition

Verb expresses:

* action
* state
* occurrence
* condition

---

# Main Verb Categories

## 1. Action Verbs

Physical/mental action.

```text
run
eat
think
write
```

Examples:

```text
She runs.
I know the answer.
```

Mental verbs are still verbs.

---

## 2. Linking Verbs

Connect subject to description.

```text
be
become
seem
appear
feel
look
```

Examples:

```text
She is smart.
He became angry.
You seem tired.
```

These do not show direct action.

---

## 3. Helping (Auxiliary) Verbs

Used to build grammar.

Main helpers:

```text
be
have
do
```

Examples:

```text
I am studying.
She has finished.
Do you know?
```

---

## Rule — English Usually Requires Verb

Wrong:

```text
I happy.
She intelligent.
```

Correct:

```text
I am happy.
She is intelligent.
```

Unlike some languages, English normally cannot omit verb.

---

# Objects

## Definition

Object receives action of verb.

```text
She kicked the ball.
```

`the ball` receives action.

---

# Types of Objects

## 1. Direct Object

Directly receives action.

```text
I ate rice.
She opened the door.
```

Questions:

* what?
* whom?

```text
I ate WHAT?
→ rice
```

---

## 2. Indirect Object

Receiver/beneficiary.

```text
She gave me money.
```

| Part  | Type            |
| ----- | --------------- |
| me    | indirect object |
| money | direct object   |

Equivalent form:

```text
She gave money to me.
```

---

# Transitive vs Intransitive Verbs

## Transitive Verbs

Need/can take object.

```text
eat
buy
kick
write
```

Examples:

```text
She bought a laptop.
He kicked the ball.
```

Sentence may feel incomplete without object.

```text
He kicked.
```

Possible but incomplete sounding.

---

## Intransitive Verbs

Do not take object.

```text
sleep
arrive
die
laugh
```

Examples:

```text
They slept.
She arrived.
```

Wrong:

```text
She arrived the station.
```

Correct:

```text
She arrived at the station.
```

---

## Ambitransitive Verbs

Can work both ways.

```text
eat
read
study
sing
```

Examples:

```text
I ate.
I ate pizza.
```

Both valid.

---

# Complements

## Definition

A complement completes meaning.
It is NOT object.

---

# Subject Complement

Describes/renames subject.

Usually after linking verbs.

```text
She is smart.
He became a doctor.
```

| Part     | Function           |
| -------- | ------------------ |
| smart    | subject complement |
| a doctor | subject complement |

---

# Object Complement

Describes object.

```text
They elected him president.
She painted the wall blue.
```

| Part      | Function       |
| --------- | -------------- |
| president | describes him  |
| blue      | describes wall |

---

# Sentence Patterns

English repeatedly uses several core patterns.

---

# 1. SV

Subject + Verb

```text
She sleeps.
Birds fly.
```

No object.

---

# 2. SVO

Subject + Verb + Object

```text
I play football.
She reads books.
```

Most common pattern.

---

# 3. SVC

Subject + Verb + Complement

```text
He is tall.
She became angry.
```

Verb links subject to description.

---

# 4. SVA

Subject + Verb + Adverbial

```text
He lives here.
She stayed at home.
```

Location/time expression completes meaning.

---

# 5. SVOO

Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object

```text
She gave me a gift.
```

Equivalent:

```text
She gave a gift to me.
```

---

# 6. SVOC

Subject + Verb + Object + Complement

```text
They called him smart.
We elected her leader.
```

Complement describes object.

---

# Word Order Rules

English relies heavily on order.

Default order:

```text
Subject → Verb → Object → Extra Information
```

Example:

```text
She bought a laptop yesterday.
```

---

# Adjective Position

Usually before noun.

```text
a red car
a smart student
```

Or after linking verbs.

```text
The car is red.
```

---

# Adverb Position

Flexible but not fully random.

Examples:

```text
She quickly ran.
She ran quickly.
```

Some positions sound more natural than others.

---

# Question Structure

English often inverts helper verb and subject.

Statement:

```text
You are ready.
```

Question:

```text
Are you ready?
```

---

# Do-Support

English uses "do" in many questions/negatives.

Statement:

```text
You like coffee.
```

Question:

```text
Do you like coffee?
```

Negative:

```text
You do not like coffee.
```

This is extremely important in English grammar.

---

# Negative Structure

Usually:

```text
Subject + helper verb + not + main verb
```

Examples:

```text
She is not ready.
I do not know.
They cannot swim.
```

---

# Important Exception — Be Verb

Be verbs do NOT use "do".

Wrong:

```text
Do you are happy?
```

Correct:

```text
Are you happy?
```

Wrong:

```text
She does not is tired.
```

Correct:

```text
She is not tired.
```

---

# English Information Flow

English usually moves from:

* known information
  → new information

Example:

```text
A man entered the room.
The man sat down.
```

First mention:

* `a man`

Second mention:

* `the man`

---

# Topic vs Grammatical Subject

Sometimes sentence topic differs from grammatical subject.

```text
As for this book, I love it.
```

Real grammatical subject:

* I

Topic:

* this book

---

# Ellipsis (Omission)

English sometimes removes repeated words.

```text
She can sing and he can too.
```

Full form:

```text
She can sing and he can sing too.
```

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

## Missing Verb

Wrong:

```text
He very tall.
```

Correct:

```text
He is very tall.
```

---

## Wrong Word Order

Wrong:

```text
Always I study.
```

Better:

```text
I always study.
```

---

## Double Subject

Wrong:

```text
My brother he works here.
```

Correct:

```text
My brother works here.
```

---

## Missing Subject

Wrong:

```text
Went to school.
```

Correct:

```text
I went to school.
```

---

# Real Core Formula

Most English grammar expands this:

```text
Subject + Verb + Information
```

Everything else modifies:

* time
* description
* emphasis
* condition
* connection
* perspective

Core skeleton remains the same.
