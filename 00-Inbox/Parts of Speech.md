# Parts of Speech — Real Usage Logic

Grammar labels are NOT enough.

You must understand:
- what the word “feels” like
- what role it plays
- where it naturally appears
- what it modifies
- why native speakers choose it

---

# 1. Nouns

## Core Feeling

A noun is:
- a thing
- a person
- a place
- an idea
- something treated like an entity

Think:

```text
"something that can exist/be talked about"
```

Examples:

```text
dog
computer
love
India
water
```

---

# How Nouns Behave

Nouns usually:
- act as subject
- act as object
- come after articles/determiners
- can be described by adjectives

---

# Common Positions

## Subject

```text
The boy runs.
```

Noun:
`boy`

---

## Object

```text
She bought a laptop.
```

Noun:
`laptop`

---

## After Prepositions

```text
on the table
in the room
under the bed
```

Prepositions are usually followed by nouns/pronouns.

---

# Countable vs Uncountable Nouns

Very important.

---

## Countable

Can count individually.

```text
book
apple
car
```

Can use:
- a/an
- many
- few
- numbers

```text
three books
many cars
a laptop
```

---

## Uncountable

Seen as mass/substance/concept.

```text
water
rice
information
advice
music
```

Usually NO:
- a/an
- plural form

Wrong:

```text
an advice
informations
```

Correct:

```text
some advice
pieces of information
```

---

# Abstract vs Concrete

## Concrete

Can physically sense.

```text
phone
dog
tree
```

---

## Abstract

Ideas/concepts.

```text
love
freedom
knowledge
```

---

# Proper vs Common Nouns

## Proper

Specific names.

```text
Faizal
India
Monday
```

Usually capitalized.

---

## Common

General category.

```text
boy
country
city
```

---

# Noun Logic

English often turns actions/qualities into nouns.

```text
decide → decision
happy → happiness
move → movement
```

English heavily uses noun forms in formal writing.

---

# 2. Pronouns

## Core Feeling

Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition.

Without pronouns:

```text
Faizal studies.
Faizal codes.
Faizal sleeps.
```

Natural English:

```text
Faizal studies.
He codes.
He sleeps.
```

---

# Main Pronouns

| Subject | Object |
|---|---|
| I | me |
| he | him |
| she | her |
| we | us |
| they | them |

---

# Subject vs Object Pronouns

## Subject Pronouns

Do action.

```text
She runs.
They study.
```

---

## Object Pronouns

Receive action.

```text
I saw him.
She called me.
```

Wrong:

```text
She called I.
```

Correct:

```text
She called me.
```

---

# It

## Real object

```text
The phone is expensive. It costs a lot.
```

---

## Dummy subject

```text
It is raining.
It is late.
```

No real object being referred to.

---

# They

Can refer to:
- plural
- singular unknown person

```text
Someone forgot their bag.
```

Very common modern English.

---

# 3. Verbs

## Core Feeling

Verb = action/state/existence/change.

Sentence engine.

Without verb:
English usually breaks.

---

# Action Verbs

Show action.

```text
run
eat
write
study
```

---

# State Verbs

Show condition/state.

```text
know
love
believe
understand
```

Usually NOT used continuously.

Wrong/unusual:

```text
I am knowing.
```

Correct:

```text
I know.
```

---

# Linking Verbs

Connect subject to description.

```text
She is smart.
He became angry.
```

No direct action transferred.

---

# Be Verbs

Most special verbs in English.

```text
am
is
are
was
were
be
been
being
```

Used for:
- existence
- identity
- description
- passive voice
- continuous tense

---

# Helping Verbs

Build grammar.

```text
do
have
be
will
can
```

Examples:

```text
I do not know.
She has finished.
They are studying.
```

---

# Verb Forms

Most verbs have forms.

Example:

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| base | eat |
| past | ate |
| past participle | eaten |
| -ing | eating |

---

# Transitive vs Intransitive

## Transitive

Action transfers to object.

```text
She kicked the ball.
```

---

## Intransitive

No object.

```text
He slept.
```

---

# Verb Position

Usually after subject.

```text
She runs.
```

Questions may invert:

```text
Is she ready?
```

---

# 4. Adjectives

## Core Feeling

Adjectives add qualities/details to nouns.

Think:

```text
"What kind?"
"Which one?"
```

---

# Before Nouns

Most common position.

```text
a red car
a smart student
```

---

# After Linking Verbs

```text
The car is red.
She seems tired.
```

---

# Adjective Order

Native speakers follow invisible order.

Natural:

```text
a beautiful small old wooden house
```

Unnatural:

```text
a wooden old small beautiful house
```

General order:

```text
opinion → size → age → shape → color → origin → material → noun
```

---

# Adjectives Do NOT Modify Verbs

Wrong:

```text
She sings beautiful.
```

Correct:

```text
She sings beautifully.
```

Need adverb there.

---

# 5. Adverbs

## Core Feeling

Adverbs modify:
- verbs
- adjectives
- other adverbs
- entire sentence

Think:

```text
"how?"
"when?"
"where?"
"to what degree?"
```

---

# Adverbs Modifying Verbs

```text
She runs quickly.
```

How does she run?
→ quickly

---

# Adverbs Modifying Adjectives

```text
very smart
extremely difficult
```

---

# Adverbs Modifying Adverbs

```text
very slowly
extremely carefully
```

---

# Sentence Adverbs

Modify whole sentence.

```text
Honestly, I disagree.
Fortunately, nobody was hurt.
```

---

# Not All Adverbs End in -ly

```text
fast
hard
late
early
```

Already adverbs.

---

# Some -ly Words Are Adjectives

```text
friendly
lovely
lonely
```

---

# Adverb Position

Flexible.

```text
She quickly ran.
She ran quickly.
```

Both valid.
But emphasis changes slightly.

---

# 6. Prepositions

## Core Feeling

Prepositions show:
- relation
- position
- direction
- time
- connection

---

# in

Core feeling:

```text
inside boundaries/container
```

Examples:

```text
in the room
in India
in water
```

Time:

```text
in 2025
in January
```

Long/general periods.

---

# on

Core feeling:

```text
touching surface
```

Examples:

```text
on the table
on the wall
```

Time:

```text
on Monday
on July 5
```

Specific day/date.

---

# at

Core feeling:

```text
point/specific location
```

Examples:

```text
at school
at home
at the station
```

Time:

```text
at 5 PM
at noon
```

Exact point in time.

---

# under

Core feeling:

```text
lower than something
```

```text
under the bed
under the bridge
```

---

# over

Core feeling:

```text
above something
```

```text
over the table
```

Can imply covering/movement too.

---

# between

Used for:
- two clear points/entities

```text
between Chennai and Madurai
```

---

# among

Used for:
- groups/many

```text
among friends
```

---

# Common Preposition Problem

English prepositions are partially logical and partially memorized.

Examples:

```text
good at
interested in
afraid of
```

These combinations must often be learned together.

---

# 7. Conjunctions

## Core Feeling

Connect ideas.

---

# and

Adds information.

```text
I studied and slept.
```

---

# but

Contrast.

```text
He is rich but unhappy.
```

---

# or

Choice/alternative.

```text
Tea or coffee?
```

---

# because

Reason.

```text
I stayed home because it rained.
```

---

# although

Unexpected contrast.

```text
Although he was tired, he worked.
```

---

# 8. Interjections

Emotion sounds/reactions.

```text
wow!
ouch!
oh!
hey!
```

Often outside grammar structure.

---

# 9. Articles & Determiners

## a / an

Non-specific singular countable noun.

```text
a cat
an apple
```

---

# a vs an

Based on SOUND, not spelling.

```text
an hour
a university
```

Because:
- hour starts with vowel sound
- university starts with "yoo"

---

# the

Specific/known thing.

```text
the car we bought
the teacher
```

Speaker/listener know which one.

---

# some

Unknown amount/plural.

```text
some water
some students
```

---

# many

Countable plural.

```text
many books
```

---

# much

Uncountable.

```text
much water
much time
```

---

# few vs little

## few

Countable.

```text
few books
```

---

## little

Uncountable.

```text
little water
```

---

# Final Understanding

Parts of speech are not just labels.

You must understand:
- what they modify
- where they appear
- what relationship they create
- what feeling they carry
- what patterns English expects

That is how grammar becomes intuitive instead of memorized.
