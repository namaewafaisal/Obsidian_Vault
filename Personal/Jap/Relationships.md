# 🇯🇵 Japanese Family & Relationship Terms

Japanese family terms are divided into **two core categories**:

1. **Terms for your own family** → humble / neutral  
2. **Terms for someone else’s family** → polite / honorific  

Correct usage depends on:
- **Perspective (in-group vs out-group)**
- **Formality**
- **Vowel length** (short vs long vowels can change meaning)

---

## 👨‍👩‍👧 General Family Term

- **家族 (かぞく / kazoku)** — family (general)
- **ご家族 (ごかぞく / gokazoku)** — *someone else’s* family (polite)

> Prefix **ご-** adds respect when referring to others.

---

## 👵 Parents & Direct Ancestors

### Father
- **父 (ちち / chichi)** — my father (when speaking to others)
- **お父さん (おとうさん / otōsan)** — someone else’s father or polite address

### Mother
- **母 (はは / haha)** — my mother
- **お母さん (おかあさん / okāsan)** — someone else’s mother / polite address

### Grandparents
- **おじいさん (ojīsan)** — grandfather  
- **おばあさん (obāsan)** — grandmother  

⚠️ **Long vowels are critical here**.

---

## 👦 Siblings (Seniority Matters)

Japanese distinguishes siblings by **age**.

### Brothers
- **兄 (あに / ani)** — older brother (my own, humble)
- **お兄さん (おにいさん / oniisan)** — someone else’s older brother / polite address
- **弟 (おとうと / otōto)** — younger brother

### Sisters
- **妹 (いもうと / imōto)** — younger sister  
*(older sister: 姉 / ane — not listed in your source, but structurally parallel)*

---

## ⚠️ Extended Family & the “Vowel Trap”

A single long vowel can change the relative entirely.

### Uncle vs Grandfather
- **おじさん (ojisan)** — uncle  
- **おじいさん (ojīsan)** — grandfather  

### Aunt vs Grandmother
- **おばさん (obasan)** — aunt  
- **おばあさん (obāsan)** — grandmother  

> **Mistake = different generation.**  
> Always exaggerate vowel length when practicing.

---

## 🏷 Social Etiquette & Honorifics

### The **-san (さん)** rule
- Used for:
  - Other people
  - Other people’s family members
- **Never used for:**
  - Yourself
  - Your own family members (when speaking to outsiders)

Using **-san** on your own family violates **humble speech norms**.

---

## 🪞 The “Family Mirror” Metaphor

Japanese family terms behave like a **double-sided mirror**:

- **Your family (in-group):**  
  → spoken of humbly  
  (*父, 母, 兄*)

- **Someone else’s family (out-group):**  
  → spoken of respectfully  
  (*お父さん, お母さん, お兄さん*)

The words change based on **social distance**, not biology.
