# 📊 Master Sheet: Ages & Averages (Intensive)

## 👨‍👩‍👦 Problems on Ages

### **1. The 5 Foundation Rules**
1. **Current Age:** If the current age is $x$, then $n$ times the age is $nx$.
2. **Future (Hence/Later):** If current age is $x$, then age $n$ years later = $x + n$.
3. **Past (Ago/Before):** If current age is $x$, then age $n$ years ago = $x - n$.
4. **Ratios:** The ages in a ratio $a : b$ are represented as $ax$ and $bx$.
5. **Fractions:** If current age is $x$, then $1/n$ of the age is $x/n$.

---

### **2. Advanced "Gap" Logic**
> [!IMPORTANT] **The Golden Rule**
> The difference between the ages of two persons is **constant** at any point in time. 

#### **Scenario A: Gaps Match (Easy)**
If the ratio is $2:3$ now and $3:4$ in **10 years**:
- Difference in Ratio 1 ($3-2$) = **1 unit**
- Difference in Ratio 2 ($4-3$) = **1 unit**
- **Conclusion:** Since the gaps are equal, the 1-unit jump ($2 \to 3$) is exactly **10 years**.

#### **Scenario B: Gaps Don't Match (The Balancing Trick)**
If the differences between ratio parts are not equal, you must **force** them to match before solving.

**The Balancing Method:**
1. Find the difference ($D_1$) of Ratio 1.
2. Find the difference ($D_2$) of Ratio 2.
3. Multiply Ratio 1 by $D_2$.
4. Multiply Ratio 2 by $D_1$.

**Example:**
- **Present:** $1:3$ (Diff $D_1 = 2$)
- **In 10 Years:** $2:5$ (Diff $D_2 = 3$)
- *Step 1:* Multiply $(1:3)$ by $D_2(3) \implies \mathbf{3:9}$ (New Diff = 6)
- *Step 2:* Multiply $(2:5)$ by $D_1(2) \implies \mathbf{4:10}$ (New Diff = 6)
- **Now solve:** The jump from $3 \to 4$ (or $9 \to 10$) is **1 unit**. 
- **1 unit = 10 years.**

## 📈 Averages & Speed

### **1. Basic Average**
> $$Average = \frac{\text{Sum of observations}}{\text{Number of observations}}$$

### **2. Special Series Shortcuts**
* **First $n$ natural numbers:** $Avg = \frac{n + 1}{2}$
* **First $n$ even numbers:** $Avg = n + 1$
* **First $n$ odd numbers:** $Avg = n$
* **Consecutive Numbers (AP):** $Avg = \frac{\text{First Term} + \text{Last Term}}{2}$

### **3. Average Speed (All Scenarios)**
* **Case 1: Two Equal Distances:** $\text{Avg Speed} = \frac{2xy}{x + y}$
* **Case 2: Three Equal Distances:** $\text{Avg Speed} = \frac{3xyz}{xy + yz + zx}$
* **Case 3: Unequal Distances:** $$\text{Avg Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{d_1 + d_2}{\frac{d_1}{v_1} + \frac{d_2}{v_2}}$$



---

## 🔄 The Replacement Formula
*Used for: “An old person leaves a group and a new one joins...”*

> [!NOTE] **The New Value Equation**
> $$\text{New Person's Age} = \text{Old Person's Age} + (\text{Number of people} \times \text{Change in Average})$$
> *Note: Use **+** if average increased, **-** if it decreased.*

# 📊 Master Sheet: Percentages, Population & Depreciation

## 💹 Concept of Percentage

### **1. The Foundation Rules**
1. **Meaning:** "Percent" literally means "per hundred." $x\%$ is represented as $\frac{x}{100}$.
2. **Percent to Fraction:** To express $x\%$ as a fraction, divide by 100.  
   * *Example:* $20\% = \frac{20}{100} = \frac{1}{5}$
3. **Fraction to Percent:** To express a fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ as a percent, multiply by 100.  
   * *Example:* $\frac{1}{4} = \left(\frac{1}{4} \times 100\right)\% = 25\%$

---

## ⚖️ Consumption & Expenditure Logic
*Goal: To keep the total expenditure constant when prices fluctuate.*

### **1. Price Increase Scenario**
If the price of a commodity increases by $R\%$, the reduction in consumption needed is:
> $$\text{Reduction \%} = \left[ \frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100 \right]\%$$

### **2. Price Decrease Scenario**
If the price of a commodity decreases by $R\%$, the increase in consumption allowed is:
> $$\text{Increase \%} = \left[ \frac{R}{100 - R} \times 100 \right]\%$$

---

## 🏘️ Results on Population
*Let $P$ be the present population and $R$ be the growth rate per annum.*

### **1. Population after $n$ years**
> $$\text{Population} = P \times \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n$$

### **2. Population $n$ years ago**
> $$\text{Population} = \frac{P}{\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n}$$

---

## 📉 Results on Depreciation
*Used for: Value of machines or equipment over time. Let $P$ be the present value.*

### **1. Value after $n$ years**
> $$\text{Value} = P \times \left(1 - \frac{R}{100}\right)^n$$

### **2. Value $n$ years ago**
> $$\text{Value} = \frac{P}{\left(1 - \frac{R}{100}\right)^n}$$



---

## 🔄 Comparison Shortcuts

### **1. A is R% more than B**
Then $B$ is less than $A$ by:
> $$\text{Less \%} = \left[ \frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100 \right]\%$$

### **2. A is R% less than B**
Then $B$ is more than $A$ by:
> $$\text{More \%} = \left[ \frac{R}{100 - R} \times 100 \right]\%$$

---

## ⚡ Successive Percentage Change
*Used for: Two consecutive changes on the same value (e.g., price increases by 10% then 20%).*
> $$\text{Net Change \%} = x + y + \frac{xy}{100}$$
> *(Note: Use positive values for increase and negative values for decrease/discount).*
