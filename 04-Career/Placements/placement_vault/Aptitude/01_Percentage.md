# 📊 Percentage

---

## 🔴 CORE CONCEPT

Percent = per hundred. x% = x/100.

**Two directions you'll always need:**
- Number → Percentage: multiply by 100
- Percentage → Number: divide by 100

---

## 🔴 FORMULAS (IMPORTANCE ORDER)

### 1. What % of X is Y?
$$\frac{Y}{X} \times 100$$

### 2. X is what % more/less than Y?
$$\frac{X - Y}{Y} \times 100$$
> Always divide by the **base** (what you're comparing to)

### 3. Successive % Change (most exam trap)
$$\text{Net \%} = a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$$
> Use + for increase, − for decrease

### 4. Price Increase → Reduce Consumption (to keep spend same)
$$\text{Reduction \%} = \frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100$$

### 5. Price Decrease → Can Increase Consumption
$$\text{Increase \%} = \frac{R}{100 - R} \times 100$$

### 6. A is R% more than B → B is less than A by:
$$\frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100$$

### 7. A is R% less than B → B is more than A by:
$$\frac{R}{100 - R} \times 100$$

## 🟠 SOLVED EXAMPLES

### Example 1 — Basic %
> A man earns ₹8000. He spends ₹6000. What % does he save?

Savings = 8000 − 6000 = 2000
% saved = (2000/8000) × 100 = **25%**

---

### Example 2 — % more/less than
> A's salary is ₹5000. B's is ₹4000. A's salary is what % more than B's?

= (5000 − 4000)/4000 × 100 = 1000/4000 × 100 = **25%**
> Note: Base is B (what you compare to)

---

### Example 3 — Successive change
> Price increases 10% then decreases 10%. Net change?

Net = 10 + (−10) + (10×−10)/100 = 0 − 1 = **−1% (net decrease)**
> Same % up and down = always a net LOSS of x²/100

---

### Example 4 — Income/expenditure savings problem
> Income increases 20%, expenditure increases 10%. Original: income ₹10000, spends 75% (₹7500). Find % increase in savings.

Original savings = 10000 − 7500 = 2500
New income = 12000, New expenditure = 7500 × 1.1 = 8250
New savings = 12000 − 8250 = 3750
% increase = (3750 − 2500)/2500 × 100 = **50%**

---

### Example 5 — Reverse percentage
> After a 20% increase, a number becomes 240. Find the original.

Original × 1.2 = 240 → Original = 240/1.2 = **200**

---

## 🟠 SOLVING APPROACH

1. **Identify the base** — what are you taking % of?
2. **Identify direction** — increase or decrease?
3. If two changes back to back → use successive formula
4. If asked "what % more/less" → always divide by the reference value
5. For savings problems → calculate absolute values first, then %

---

## 🟡 PRACTICE PROBLEMS

**P1.** A number is increased by 25% and then decreased by 20%. Net change?

**P2.** If 30% of A = 40% of B, find A:B.

**P3.** In an election, candidate A gets 55% of votes. Candidate B gets 45%. A wins by 1800 votes. Total votes?

**P4.** A man spends 80% of his income. His income increases 25%, spending increases 10%. % increase in savings?

**P5.** Price of oil rises 25%. By what % must consumption be reduced to keep expenditure same?

**P6.** A is 20% more than B. B is what % less than A?

**P7.** Population of a town is 10000. Grows 10% year 1, 20% year 2. Population after 2 years?

**P8.** Out of 500 students, 60% are boys. 40% of boys and 50% of girls passed. How many passed?

---

## ❌ COMMON MISTAKES

- Dividing by wrong base in more/less questions
- Using arithmetic mean instead of successive formula for back-to-back %
- Forgetting net successive change is always a loss when same % up then down
