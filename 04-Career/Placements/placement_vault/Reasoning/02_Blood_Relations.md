## 🔴 THE 3 QUESTION TYPES

### Type 1 — Direct chain
Given step-by-step relationships. Build the tree.

### Type 2 — Coded relations
Symbols replace words. Decode first, then build tree.

### Type 3 — Pointing/introducing
Someone points to a person and describes them. Work backwards.

---

## 🔴 KEY TRICKS

### "Only daughter/son" trick
- "Only daughter of my mother" = **the speaker herself**
- "Only son of my grandfather" = **my father**
- Resolve these immediately before continuing

### Gender from context
- If someone is a "husband" → male
- If someone is a "wife" → female
- If "brother/sister" of a known gender → determine accordingly

### In-law generation
- Father-in-law = spouse's father (same generation as parent)
- Brother-in-law = spouse's brother OR sister's husband

## 🟠 SOLVED EXAMPLES

### Example 1 — Direct chain
> A is father of B. B is sister of C. C is mother of D. A related to D?

A → father of B
B → sister of C (same generation as B, so C is A's child too)
C → mother of D
So A is D's **grandfather**

---

### Example 2 — Coded relations
> P÷Q = P is mother of Q. P×Q = P is husband of Q. P+Q = P is sister of Q.
> A÷B×C+D. How is A related to D?

A÷B → A is mother of B
B×C → B is husband of C (B is male, C is female)
C+D → C is sister of D
Tree: A is mother of B. B and C are married. C is sister of D.
A is mother of B → A is also mother-in-law of C → C's sister D → A is D's **mother-in-law's mother? No.**

Actually: A→B's mother. C is B's wife. D is C's sibling.
A is B's mother → A is C's mother-in-law → A is D's **mother-in-law** (if D is also married to someone related) — No.

Simpler: A is B's mother. C is sister of D. B married C.
A related to D = A is the **mother-in-law's** relation? No — D is just C's sibling.
A is **mother of B who is husband of C who is sister of D** → A is D's **brother's mother-in-law** — that's too complex.
Standard answer for this type: A is D's **mother-in-law** (A is the mother of D's sister's husband — so A is the mother-in-law of C, who is D's sister. Relationship to D = aunt-equivalent but standard answer = **cannot be directly named / maternal aunt-in-law**).

> Practical approach: Draw the tree on paper, trace the path.

---

### Example 3 — Pointing type
> "His mother is the only daughter of my mother." How is woman related to the man?

Only daughter of my mother = the woman herself
So: His mother = the woman
Woman is the man's **mother**

---

### Example 4 — Negative/trick
> "Pointing to a man, woman says 'He is the son of my father's only son.'"

My father's only son = the woman's brother (or herself if no brothers — but "son" implies male → her brother)
He is the son of her brother → He is her **nephew**

---

### Example 5 — Gender trap
> "If A is the brother of B, B is the sister of C, C is the father of D — how is A related to D?"

A = B's brother → A is male
B = C's sister → C is male (father of D)
A's generation = B's generation = C's generation
A is C's brother-in-law? No — B is C's sister, A is B's brother.
If A, B, C are all siblings: A is D's **uncle**

## 🟠 SOLVING APPROACH

1. Draw a tree immediately — never solve in your head
2. Assign gender as soon as you know it
3. Resolve "only son/daughter" phrases immediately
4. Work from known person outward
5. For coded relations — decode all symbols before building tree

---

## 🟡 PRACTICE PROBLEMS

**P1.** Pointing to a girl, a boy says "She is the daughter of the only son of my grandfather." How is the girl related to the boy?

**P2.** A is B's sister. C is B's mother. D is C's father. E is D's mother. How is A related to D?

**P3.** If X+Y means X is father of Y, X−Y means X is wife of Y, X×Y means X is brother of Y.
P+Q−R×S. How is P related to S?

**P4.** Pointing to a man in a photograph, a woman says "His mother is the only daughter-in-law of my mother." How is the woman related to the man?

**P5.** A man says "The woman I married is the daughter of the only son of my grandmother." Who is the woman to the man?

**P6.** If A is the son of B, C is A's sister, D is B's mother, and E is D's husband — how many male members are there if we know B is female?

---

## ❌ COMMON MISTAKES

- Assuming gender when not stated
- Missing that "only daughter of my mother" = the speaker
- Confusing nephew/niece (nephew = brother's/sister's son, niece = their daughter)
- Not drawing the tree — solving blind leads to errors
