# 8.6 — PERT

PERT:
> project scheduling technique used when task durations are uncertain.

Uses:
- optimistic estimate
- most likely estimate
- pessimistic estimate

---

# Three Time Estimates

| Estimate | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Optimistic (O) | Best-case duration |
| Most Likely (M) | Normal expected duration |
| Pessimistic (P) | Worst-case duration |

---

# Expected Time Formula

```text
TE = (O + 4M + P) / 6
```

Where:
- TE = Expected Time
- O = Optimistic
- M = Most Likely
- P = Pessimistic

---

# Example

```text
O = 2
M = 5
P = 8
```

```text
TE = (2 + 4(5) + 8) / 6
   = 5 days
```

---

# PERT Characteristics

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Probabilistic | Handles uncertainty |
| Uses 3 estimates | O, M, P |
| Network-based | Models dependencies |
| Risk-aware | Useful for uncertain projects |

---

# PERT vs CPM

| PERT | CPM |
|---|---|
| Uncertain durations | Fixed durations |
| Probability-based | Deterministic |
| Estimation-focused | Scheduling-focused |

---

# Advantages

- handles uncertainty better
- useful for complex projects
- improves schedule estimation

---

# Disadvantages

- estimation may still be inaccurate
- calculations more complex
- depends on estimation quality

---

# Common Uses

- research projects
- large software projects
- uncertain development environments

---

# Common Mistakes

- confusing PERT with CPM
- forgetting most likely estimate has weight 4

---

# Important Insight

PERT estimates:
> project schedules under uncertainty using probabilistic task durations
