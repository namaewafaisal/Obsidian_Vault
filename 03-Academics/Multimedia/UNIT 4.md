## Group 1 – Animation Fundamentals

> Big Question:
>
> How is motion created in multimedia?

### Animation

Animation is the process of creating the illusion of movement by displaying a sequence of images rapidly.

Persistence of vision makes the human eye perceive continuous motion.

Typical frame rates:

* 24 FPS → Movies
* 30 FPS → Videos
* 60 FPS → Games

---

## Types of Animation

### 2D Animation

Objects move in two dimensions:

* Width
* Height

Examples:

* Cartoons
* Motion graphics

---

### 2.5D Animation

2D elements are arranged to create a 3D effect.

Examples:

* Side-scrolling games
* Parallax websites

---

### 3D Animation

Objects move in three dimensions:

* Width
* Height
* Depth

Examples:

* Movies
* Games
* Simulations

Developed by Disney animators.

### The 12 Principles

1. Squash and Stretch
2. Anticipation
3. Staging
4. Straight Ahead Action and Pose-to-Pose
5. Follow Through and Overlapping Action
6. Slow In and Slow Out
7. Arcs
8. Secondary Action
9. Timing
10. Exaggeration
11. Solid Drawing
12. Appeal

---

### Important Principles

#### Squash and Stretch

Gives objects flexibility and weight.

Example:

* Bouncing ball

---

#### Anticipation

Prepares viewers for an action.

Example:

* Character bends before jumping.

---

#### Timing

Controls speed and emotion.

More frames → Slower movement

Fewer frames → Faster movement

---

#### Appeal

Makes characters engaging.

## Group 3 – Core Animation Techniques

### Keyframe Animation

Keyframes define important positions.

Software automatically generates intermediate frames.

These intermediate frames are called:

* Tween frames

Example:

```text id="keyframe"
Frame 1 → Ball at top

Frame 30 → Ball at bottom
```

Software creates frames 2–29.

---

### Onion Skinning

Displays previous and next frames transparently.

Helps animators create smooth motion.

---

### Morphing

Smooth transformation of one object into another.

Examples:

* Face transformation effects
* Logo animations

---

### Stop-Motion Animation

Physical objects are moved slightly and photographed frame by frame.

Examples:

* Clay animation
* Puppet animation

---

### Motion Graphics

Animation of text and graphic elements.

Examples:

* Title sequences
* Advertisements

---

## Group 4 – Rigging and Inverse Kinematics

### Character Rigging

Rigging creates a digital skeleton for a 3D model.

Components:

* Bones
* Joints
* Controllers

Purpose:

* Simplifies animation

---

## Rigging Workflow

```text id="rigging"
3D Model
    ↓
Skeleton
    ↓
Controls
    ↓
Animation
```

---

### Inverse Kinematics (IK)

IK automatically calculates joint movement based on the desired position.

Example:

Move the hand → elbow and shoulder adjust automatically.

---

### Forward Kinematics (FK) vs Inverse Kinematics (IK)

| Feature    | FK              | IK                  |
| ---------- | --------------- | ------------------- |
| Control    | Parent to child | Child to parent     |
| Complexity | Simple          | Complex             |
| Best For   | Natural motion  | Precise positioning |

## Group 5 – Virtual Reality and Augmented Reality

### Virtual Reality (VR)

VR creates a completely immersive digital environment.

Devices:

* VR headsets
* Motion controllers

Examples:

* Flight simulators
* VR games

---

### Augmented Reality (AR)

AR overlays digital content onto the real world.

Devices:

* Smartphones
* AR glasses

Examples:

* Pokémon GO
* Snapchat filters

---

## VR vs AR

| Feature     | VR               | AR               |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| Environment | Fully virtual    | Real + virtual   |
| Immersion   | High             | Moderate         |
| Hardware    | VR headset       | Smartphone       |
| User View   | Replaces reality | Enhances reality |
